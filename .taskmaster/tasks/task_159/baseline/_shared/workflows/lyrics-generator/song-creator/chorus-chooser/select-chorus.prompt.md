## How to Judge

**Step 0 — Hard filter on negative constraints (do this FIRST).** Scan the creative direction, architecture, and material palette for explicit prohibitions — phrases framed as "no [X]," "avoid [X]," "do not [X]," "would kill this song if [Y]," or any discipline the concept imposes about setting, imagery domains, characters, or actions the song must not contain. **Disqualify any chorus that violates one of these, no matter how strong its craft.** A chorus that breaks the concept's discipline cannot win — even a runner-up slot is only valid if no undisqualified option remains. State each disqualification explicitly at the top of your ranking ("Option N disqualified: violates '[quoted constraint]'") before you rank the rest. If every option violates a hard constraint, say so and pick the least-violating as a last resort — do not silently rank them as normal.

The scoring rubric already checked hook strength, genre fit, singability, meaning evolution, craft, emotional payoff, and narrator fidelity (whether the chorus preserves the narrator's psychological frame — especially important for concepts built on dramatic irony where the narrator must NOT confess their blind spot). A chorus that scored high on emotional payoff but low on narrator fidelity is usually a line where the narrator is being too self-aware for the concept. Weight the narrator fidelity scores alongside the other dimensions — a chorus can be craft-strong and still be structurally wrong for the narrator's frame. You're looking for something beyond the rubric:

- **The stranger test (MOST IMPORTANT)**: Imagine someone walks into a room mid-song and hears ONLY this chorus with no context. Do they feel something? Can they hum it after? If a chorus requires the verses to make sense, or requires knowledge of the concept to land, it fails as a chorus. A chorus must work ALONE. "Check. Neck. Heavy. Check." is a sound experiment, not a chorus. "Limestone, lintel, Indiana lung / A long-earned rest for a heavy tongue" works alone — you feel weight, rest, and earthiness without needing anything else.
- **Gut reaction**: Which chorus hits you first? Which one are you still thinking about after reading all eight?
- **The song test**: Imagine this chorus coming back three times in a full song. Which one gains the most weight through repetition?
- **The artist test**: If you handed this chorus to a real artist in this genre, which one would they be most excited to build a song around?
