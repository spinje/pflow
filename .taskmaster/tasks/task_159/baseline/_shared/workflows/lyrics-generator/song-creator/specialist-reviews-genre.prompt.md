## What You're Checking

### Vocabulary Register
Every genre has a vocabulary world. Country uses different words than post-punk. Hip-hop has different rhythmic density than folk. Check:
- Do the word choices feel natural for this genre, or imported from somewhere else?
- Does the vocabulary level (literary vs. conversational vs. raw vs. clever) match the genre's norms?
- Are there words that would sound wrong coming out of an artist in this genre?

### Structural Conventions
Each genre has structural expectations:
- Does the verse-chorus relationship work for this genre? (Pop wants hooky choruses. Country wants story-driven verses. Punk wants urgency throughout.)
- Is the chorus structured appropriately? (Anthemic for arena rock, catchy and melodic for pop, raw for punk, groove-based for R&B)
- Does the song length and density feel right?

### Rhythmic Feel
Read the lyrics aloud. Do the syllable patterns FEEL like this genre?
- Pop: tends toward regular, predictable syllable counts and natural phrasing
- Hip-hop/rap: dense, varied, rhythmically complex, internal rhyme heavy
- Folk/country: conversational pacing, story-paced, breathing room
- Punk: short, sharp, urgent, minimal syllables
- R&B: flowing, longer phrases, open vowels on sustained moments
- Indie: varies widely, but often values unexpected phrasing over conventional flow

### Emotional Register
How does this genre typically handle emotion?
- Country: direct, unguarded, specific storytelling
- Indie: oblique, image-driven, understated, sometimes detached
- Pop: universal, accessible, emotionally clear
- Post-punk/art rock: intellectual, angular, tension between cool and raw
- Folk: intimate, confessional, detailed
- Does this song match the genre's emotional conventions?

### Singability & Musical Structure
Would these lyrics work SUNG in this genre? Assess both the vocal delivery and whether the lyrics achieve the musical structure designed for them.

**Vocal delivery:**
- Does the phrasing feel speakable/singable, or does it read like prose forced into lines?
- Are there awkward consonant clusters on words that need to be sustained or belted?
- Are open vowels placed where the melody would need to soar or sustain?
- Do line lengths allow for natural breathing?
- Do any sections have lines that feel unnaturally uniform or contorted, as if shaped to serve a hidden pattern rather than natural expression?

**Structure against the blueprint:**
The song architecture below defines musical structure targets for each section — line count, syllable range, and rhyme scheme — designed for this genre. Check two things:

1. **Are the targets genre-appropriate?** If 14-syllable lines were specified for a punk song, the architect got it wrong — flag the target itself as a genre mismatch. The targets should feel like natural structural expectations for this genre.
2. **Do the lyrics hit the targets?** Check each section's syllable range, line count, and rhyme scheme against the design. Where did the writer hit them and where did they drift?

**Trust your ear over the numbers.** A line within the target that's unsingable is still a problem. A line outside the target that flows naturally in performance might be fine. The question is always "would this work performed?" — the numbers are reference points, not rules.

**Deliberate exceptions are allowed.** A breathless run, a spoken-word break, a deliberately truncated line mirroring loss — if a structural deviation serves an artistic purpose, flag it as an observation (noting what it achieves), not a problem. Only flag deviations that genuinely weaken singability or genre feel.
