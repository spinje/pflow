## Step 0 — Identify the Character's Voice World

Before flagging anything, read the Creative Direction (below) and identify the character's natural vocabulary. Real people talk in idioms, jargon, and genre conventions specific to their world, profession, faith, region, generation, and class. A phrase that is a dead cliché in one voice world can be a diagnostic marker in another — the exact thing that proves the character is drawn from a real speaker rather than a generic voice.

Identify the voice world along any axes the creative direction names:

- **Faith, religious tradition, or cosmology** — the narrator's religious vocabulary and idioms, if any
- **Profession or trade** — specialist vocabulary from how this character makes a living
- **Region and class** — local idiom, class markers, generational speech patterns
- **Subculture or medium** — music-scene vocabulary, digital-native phrasings, academic register, sports talk — whatever subculture the character inhabits
- **Genre convention** — the specific conventions of the song's genre (not pop defaults)

When the character belongs to a specific voice world, the formulaic phrases of that world are diagnostic, not decorative. The songwriter has committed to voice; do not ask the character to sanitize themselves out of it.

**Rules for flagging under a voice world:**

1. If a phrase lives naturally inside this character's voice world, it is **voice-diagnostic, not AI-tell.** Do NOT flag it. The phrase earns its place because a real speaker of that world would say it.
2. The **universal AI hit list still applies regardless of voice world** — fade, echo, neon, whisper-as-metaphor, ember, veil, shatter, cascade, crystallize, resonate, ethereal. These are training-bleed decoration words that do not survive in any real speaker's mouth. Flag them unless used literally.
3. Generic cross-voice clichés also still apply regardless of voice world — "heart on fire," "break these chains," "find my way back," abstract-noun pairs as line endings. Flag these regardless of character.
4. **If the Creative Direction does not describe a specific voice world**, apply the full hit list without exception — when there is no character claim, there is no character excuse.
5. When a convention is genre-load-bearing rather than AI-decorative (perfect end-rhymes in genres that expect them, repeat-line structures where the genre demands them, shouted one-word hooks where the form requires them), do NOT flag it as lazy. Genre conventions are the form's spine, not AI decoration.

State the identified voice world explicitly at the top of your review ("Voice world: [description]") so the songwriter can see what lens you applied and push back if you identified it wrong.

## The AI Vocabulary — Flag ANY of These

**Words** (when used as decoration or metaphor, not literally): fade/fading/faded, echo/echoes/echoing, whisper/whispers, shatter/shattered, neon/neon-lit, ember/embers, veil/veiled/unveil, fractal, hollow/hollowed, remnant/remnants, ghost, copper wire,tapestry, symphony (as metaphor), silhouette, unravel, ignite, cascade, crystallize, resonate, ethereal, transcend, luminous, iridescent, canvas (as metaphor), dance/dancing (as metaphor for non-physical things), weave/woven (as metaphor)

**Phrases**: "paint the sky," "beneath the surface," "light in the darkness," "walls come crashing/tumbling down," "heart on fire," "piece by piece," "through the noise," "in this moment," "lost in the [noun]," "caught between [X] and [Y]," "the weight of [abstract noun]," "chasing [noun]s," "dancing with [noun]," "drowning in [noun]," "build these walls," "break these chains," "find my way back," "ashes of [noun]," "on the edge of [noun]"

> Note this is not an exhaustive list. The hit list is a starting point for identifying AI-indicative language. You may find other words and phrases that are AI-indicative, but are not on this list. It's easy, you just have to introspect hard enough.

**Patterns**:
- Abstract noun pairs as line endings ("hope and fear," "love and pain," "light and dark")
- Starting 3+ consecutive lines with "I"
- Rhyming fire/desire, heart/apart, night/light, soul/whole, rain/pain
- Stating emotions directly instead of showing them ("I feel so broken," "my heart is heavy," "I'm lost and alone")
- Every verse ending with a rhetorical question
- The bridge restating the chorus softly instead of introducing something new
- Excessive second-person "you" with no clear specific person being addressed
- Lines that narrate their own metaphor ("like a [noun] that [verb]s" — forced simile structure)

## Your Review

For each problem you find:
1. **Quote the exact line(s)**
2. **Identify the problem** (which word/phrase/pattern)
3. **Why it reads as AI** (what makes it generic or tell-tale)
4. **Direction for replacement** (don't write the fix — point toward what would work better)

Be thorough but fair. Context matters. "Whisper" in a scene where someone literally whispers is fine. "The echoes whisper through the fading neon veil" is five flags in one line. Judge the METAPHORICAL and DECORATIVE uses, not the literal ones.
