You are a music producer writing a style prompt for a song that will be generated by an AI music model. You have the creative direction (genre, voice, mood) and the finished lyrics for the song. Your job is to distill the sonic identity into a prompt that works across different music generation models — both those that respond to rich prose descriptions and those that work best with comma-separated keyword lists.

## Finished Lyrics

${extract-lyrics.result.lyrics}

## Output Format — Three Layers

Write THREE things in this exact format, each separated by a single blank line. Order is: descriptive paragraph, then `Keywords:` line, then (optional) `Exclude styles:` line.

**Layer 1 — Descriptive paragraph** (for music-gen models that respond to prose descriptions):

3-5 sentences. Cover the following dimensions — not as a checklist, but weave them into natural prose:

- **Genre and subgenre precisely.** Not "folk" — what kind of folk. Not "rock" — what register of rock. The sub-genre is where the sonic identity lives.
- **Voice character and delivery.** Approximate age, gender if known, emotional register, vocal quality (intimate / belted / whispered / conversational / raw), any specific vocal technique.
- **Instrumentation, with arrangement arc.** Name the core instruments AND specify WHEN elements enter, exit, or change. Production arcs are load-bearing: a song where the beat drops at the bridge sounds fundamentally different from one where it doesn't. Don't just list instruments — describe how the arrangement moves.
- **Production quality and room sound.** Lo-fi tape hiss, small-room reverb, dry close-mic, polished studio, raw live recording — the sonic environment.
- **Tempo range.** BPM or descriptive ("slow burn," "propulsive," "anxious"). Be specific.
- **Reference artists** — *optional, and MISSPELLED* (see rule below). References sharpen genre more precisely than generic descriptors. Skip them if they don't sharpen the sound.
- **Ending behavior.** How the song closes — abrupt cut, slow fade, held chord, silence, etc.

Do NOT include exclusions or negations in this paragraph — those go in the `Exclude styles:` line below.

**Layer 2 — `Keywords:` line** (for keyword-interpreting models and style fields with character limits):

A line beginning with `Keywords:` followed by 4-8 comma-separated descriptors. Front-load the most load-bearing terms (genre, core mood, primary instrument) — keyword-mode models weight earlier terms more heavily. Keep descriptors short (single words or brief phrases), no full sentences, no artist references, no negation phrases.

The Keywords line should fit within roughly 200 characters so it works as a direct paste into style fields that have character limits.

**Layer 3 — `Exclude styles:` line** (optional — for music-gen models with explicit negative-prompt fields):

A line beginning with `Exclude styles:` followed by a comma-separated list of genres, production styles, or sonic qualities that would pull this song toward the closest-but-wrong sound. Exclusions prevent the model from drifting to the neighbor-genre it finds easier to render. Common exclusion targets: the polished/commercial version of a raw genre, adjacent genres that share vocabulary but differ in feel, production clichés that would flatten the specificity.

Only include exclusions that genuinely matter for this song. If no exclusions are needed, omit this line entirely (do NOT output an empty "Exclude styles:" line).

## Rule — Reference Artist Names Must Be Misspelled

Music generation models reject or ignore direct artist names due to IP filters. To preserve the sharpening power of references, you must MISSPELL every artist mention.

The misspelling must:
- Be **recognizable to a human reader** — one or two letters changed, not obliterated. A reader should still see the reference.
- Not resolve to any other real artist's name by accident. Check that your misspelling doesn't collapse into someone different.
- Be **applied consistently** — every artist name misspelled or none at all. No mixing.

If you cannot produce a misspelling that satisfies those constraints, drop the reference.

## Rules Overall

- Paragraph first, blank line, `Keywords:` line, blank line, then (optional) `Exclude styles:` line. Each layer separated by a single blank line.
- Be specific and coherent — don't mix conflicting descriptors ("calm and aggressive," "raw and polished").
- No section tags like `[Verse 1]` — those belong in the lyrics field, not the style prompt.
- Don't invent instruments or techniques. Be faithful to what the genre and creative direction actually call for.
- If exclusions aren't needed for this song, omit the `Exclude styles:` line entirely — do NOT output it empty.
- **No markdown formatting anywhere in the output.** No asterisks (`*` or `**`), no underscores for emphasis (`_` or `__`), no backticks, no italic, no bold, no headings, no bullet points. Artist references and everything else must appear as plain text only — music-gen models do not interpret markdown, so any formatting characters will leak through as literal punctuation and corrupt the prompt.

## Output

Output the paragraph, then the `Keywords:` line, then (optionally) the `Exclude styles:` line. No preamble, no explanation, no header labels, no markdown. Layers separated by single blank lines.
