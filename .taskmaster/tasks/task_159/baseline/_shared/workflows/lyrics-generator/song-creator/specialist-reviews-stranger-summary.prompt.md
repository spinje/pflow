## Legibility Verdict

After answering, give a one-line verdict:

**LEGIBILITY: PARTIAL** — some dimensions land (e.g., mood, imagery) but the listener can't confidently state what happens or who is involved.
**LEGIBILITY: OPAQUE** — the listener can feel a mood but cannot say what the song is about, what happens, or who the characters are.

Then in 2-4 sentences: what specifically would need to be added, named, or set up to move this song toward CLEAR? Be concrete — point to which section needs what (a named referent, a setup line for a character who currently appears without one, a line that specifies what a named tension is actually about), not vague notes like "add more context."

## Rules

- Judge only the lyrics. Do NOT fold in genre convention, common AI training patterns, or what songs like this usually mean.
- A song does not need to be a plotted story to earn CLEAR. A static confession can be CLEAR if the situation and stakes are legible. A moving scene can be OPAQUE if we can't tell who the actors are.
- Mystery is not the same as vagueness. Mystery is *questions the listener knows to ask*. Vagueness is *the listener not knowing enough to have questions*. Call the difference honestly.
- **Legibility is a floor, not a ceiling.** CLEAR does not mean the song spells out its causes, names its politics, explains its metaphors, or resolves its tensions. CLEAR means a listener can identify the situation, the actors, and the nature of the stakes from the lyrics alone. The Unsaid, the Blind Spot, and the unresolved tension are often the POINT of the song — do not ask the song to eliminate them. If a song has named the fact of a rupture or loss, and the listener can tell what kind of thing has broken, but the *cause* of that rupture is withheld, that is often CLEAR — the listener knows what to ask about, which is what legibility means. Reserve your "move toward CLEAR" suggestions for cases where the listener genuinely cannot identify who, what, or what-kind-of-conflict — not for cases where the song has chosen not to name *why*. Over-explanation requests will strip the subtext out of songs that deliberately withhold. Do not do this.
- Some songs intentionally refuse to name their subject (spiritual, abstract, sound-experiment). If the lyrics clearly SIGNAL that refusal ("this is not for you to understand"), that's a deliberate artistic choice — note it and grade PARTIAL rather than OPAQUE.
- **Flag source-dependency.** If a line or phrase seems to reference a specific piece of outside material — a real named person the listener cannot place, a news event, a proper noun with no context, a phrase that reads like a footnote to a transcript the song was built from — flag it. A song that is a tribute, a reference, or a commentary on source material the listener doesn't have is a legibility failure, no matter how elegant. Note specifically: "Line X appears to reference outside material that isn't in the song."
