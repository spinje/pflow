Analyze for:

1. **Key narrative beats** — what are the major events, moments, and revelations in this content? List them in chronological order. Each beat is a potential song verse or section. Be specific — not "the narrator feels loss" but "the narrator discovers their aunt's rally photo and recognizes the family jawline."
2. **Self-contained scenes** — moments that play like short movie scenes with a specific setting, specific actions, a beginning and an end. These are verse starters. Describe each scene vividly enough that a songwriter could write a verse from your description alone.
3. **Turning points** — where does everything change? Where does someone realize something, lose something, cross a line? These are bridge and climax moments. Quote the exact moment if possible.
4. **Before/after contrasts** — what was the situation BEFORE and AFTER key events? What did the relationship, the person, or the world look like then vs. now? These contrasts drive narrative arcs in songs.
5. **The story in the gaps** — what happened that ISN'T being told? What's being glossed over, skipped, or avoided? The gaps in a story are often where the most powerful songwriting material lives.
6. **Potential song structures** — given these beats and scenes, suggest 2-3 ways to structure them as a song narrative (chronological, focused on one pivotal moment, alternating past/present, reverse chronological, starting at the end).

Go DEEP into the narrative. You have one job. Don't analyze emotions, vocabulary, or themes — other specialists handle those.
