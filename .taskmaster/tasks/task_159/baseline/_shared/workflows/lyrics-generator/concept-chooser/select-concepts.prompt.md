## How to Judge

### The Hard Gate (eliminates before scoring)

**Character and want.** Every concept MUST have a specific person who WANTS, FEARS, or NEEDS something. Not a vague "someone experiences an atmosphere" — a CHARACTER with a DESIRE or a LOSS. Test it: can you complete the sentence "[Character] wants [specific thing] but [specific obstacle]"? If you can't, the concept fails regardless of how clever, atmospheric, or well-written it is.

An atmosphere is not a song. An observation is not a song. A process description is not a song. A song is a person who wants something they can't have, expressed through imagery the listener can feel.

Concepts that fail this gate go to the BOTTOM of the ranking, even if they're beautifully written.

### Scoring (for concepts that pass the gate)

1. **The stranger test**: If someone who knows NOTHING about the source material heard this song, would they feel something? Or would they think "I don't get it"?
2. **The replay test**: Is this a song someone would listen to once and forget, or put on repeat? Clever songs get one listen. Songs that make you FEEL something get a hundred.
3. **The pitch test**: Does the listener pitch actually make you want to hear the song? Or is it describing something clever rather than something moving?
4. **Emotional immediacy**: Does the emotional core land in the gut, or in the head? "That's how I feel" beats "that's a clever metaphor" every time.
5. **Emotional depth over topical breadth**: Prefer concepts that go DEEP on one human experience over concepts that skim a topic. A source may contain many threads — a concept built around the deepest emotional vein will produce a better song than one that cleverly references a surface detail. A concept about "bystander guilt" is deeper than a concept about "airport anxiety."
